Aabshar Sanitary: one site, two doors

A shop for homeowners and a wholesale portal for shop owners, on top of a manufacturer's brand site. This page is my decision after studying how the big sanitary brands and trade suppliers sell online.

The decision

Build one website with three parts, not three separate websites

Every strong site I studied keeps retail and trade under one brand and one catalogue. Only the price, the order size and the checkout change after a dealer logs in. Separate sites split your SEO, double the catalogue work and confuse buyers.

Door 1 · publicRetail shopOpen prices, single-piece orders, cart, Cash on Delivery, JazzCash, Easypaisa, WhatsApp order button.
Door 2 · loginDealer portalApproved shop owners only. Slab prices by carton, MOQ, quick order by SKU, reorder, ledger and credit limit.
Brand layerManufacturer pagesFactory story, quality tests, warranty registration, catalogue PDF, dealer locator, "Become a dealer" form.

Pakistan fit

What the foreign sites miss for our market

  • Cash on Delivery first, then JazzCash, Easypaisa and bank transfer with receipt upload. Card payment can wait.
  • WhatsApp on every product: "Order on WhatsApp" sends the SKU and quantity. Many buyers trust a chat more than a cart.
  • Urdu toggle for dealers in smaller cities. Product names stay in English, with Urdu labels for menus and buttons.
  • Local product names: Muslim shower, bib cock, pillar cock, angle valve, PPR pipe, commode, as people search for them.
  • Dealer credit (udhaar) ledger: shop owners buy on credit. The portal shows balance, limit and payments, like their khata.
  • Delivery by city: Cargo or company van. Show delivery charge and days by city before checkout.

Key flows

How each buyer moves through the site

Retail customer

1Search or browse→2Product page→3Cart→4Phone + address→5COD or wallet→6SMS + WhatsApp confirm

Guest checkout. No forced sign-up. Account created from phone number after the order.

New dealer

1Apply: shop name, city, CNIC, NTN (optional), shop photo→2Admin checks and sets tier + credit→3SMS: account active→4Login with OTP

Same 3-step pattern as Tradelink's trade portal. Prices stay hidden until approval.

Returning dealer

1Login→2Reorder or quick order→3Slab price applies→4On credit or bank transfer→5Dispatch + invoice in ledger
Build order

Three phases

Phase 1: Sell online

~4–6 weeks
  • Brand home, about, contact
  • Catalogue, categories, filters, product page
  • Retail cart, COD, WhatsApp order
  • Become-a-dealer form
  • Admin: products, orders, retail prices

Phase 2: Dealer portal

~4 weeks
  • Dealer login (OTP), approval
  • Tier A/B/C slab pricing, MOQ, cartons
  • Quick order, Excel upload, reorder
  • Price list PDF
  • JazzCash / Easypaisa / bank receipt

Phase 3: Run the business

~4 weeks
  • Credit ledger, payments, statements
  • Stock by warehouse, dispatch by city
  • Warranty registration and claims
  • Dealer locator, Urdu toggle
  • Sales reports
Look and feel

Design direction

Clean porcelain white, deep pipe-navy for trust, and one brass accent taken from the fittings themselves. Product photos sit on a light grey ground so chrome, black and gold finishes all read well. Dealer screens use a water-teal tint so a shop owner always knows he is in the trade view.

Tech stack I recommend: Next.js storefront + Supabase (Postgres) for products, dealers, price tiers and ledger. Shopify's B2B tools need its most expensive plan, and WooCommerce B2B plugins get slow with tier pricing on thousands of SKUs.

How many products (SKUs) at launch: about 50, 500 or 5,000?
